**Abstract**

The democratization of generative artificial intelligence relies on deploying Small Language Models (SLMs) within constrained computational environments. However, when processing dense academic corpora of 50 to 100 complex papers, traditional single-pass Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) pipelines fail due to noise injection, context fragmentation, and the "Lost in the Middle" (LIM) syndrome, where models fail to access information buried in the middle of long contexts. Furthermore, scaling inference-time compute via advanced agentic reasoning loops—such as Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS)—typically requires massive Key-Value (KV) caches that instantly exceed consumer-grade 6–20 GB Video RAM (VRAM) constraints. 

In this paper, we propose a novel end-to-end architecture that bridges the gap between algorithmic reasoning loops and physical hardware limits. Algorithmicly, we introduce **Confidence-Gated Discrimination**, a mechanism that fuses the self-play exploration of MCTS-RAG with a forward-looking active retrieval threshold ($\theta = 0.1$) to dynamically bypass expensive reasoning rollouts and discriminator verifications when the generator model is highly confident. To physically fit this architecture into a 20GB VRAM budget, we deploy a dual-SLM setup (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B and Phi-4-mini) utilizing INT4 static weight quantization alongside PolarQuant, which compresses the KV cache to 2.5–3.5 bits per channel via polar coordinate transformation. For external retrieval, we utilize TurboQuant but explicitly bypass its 1-bit Quantized Johnson-Lindenstrauss (QJL) transform in favor of raw Mean Squared Error (MSE) quantization, prioritizing retrieval stability over mathematical unbiasedness at ultra-low bit-widths. Evaluated on the NeedleChain and LongBench v2 benchmarks, our pipeline effectively flattens the LIM U-shaped degradation curve and maintains unbroken inferential chains without relying on parametric memorization, establishing a new state-of-the-art for local, multi-hop academic reasoning.

**Key Contributions**

**1. Algorithmic Innovation: Confidence-Gated Discrimination**
We introduce a hybrid reasoning loop that fundamentally optimizes the state-space explosion of traditional tree-search algorithms. By fusing the discrete action space of MCTS-RAG (including specific actions for *Retrieval Reasoning* and *Retrieval Decompose*) with the active retrieval threshold logic of FLARE, we successfully gate the tree expansion process. Specifically, the computationally expensive 3.8B discriminator (Phi-4-mini) and the MCTS rollouts are entirely bypassed if the 7B generator’s (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen) token confidence exceeds $\theta = 0.1$. Furthermore, we strictly bound the search depth to $d=5$ and restrict high-variance expansions to a maximum of 5 nodes per depth level to guarantee stable, low-latency generation. 

**2. Systems-Level Hardware Optimization for Agentic RAG**
We provide a comprehensive blueprint for escaping the Out-of-Memory (OOM) trap inherent to long-context reasoning on edge hardware (6-20GB VRAM). Our architecture successfully stacks INT4 static weight quantization (reducing the dual-SLM footprint to ~6-8GB) with dynamic KV cache compression via PolarQuant. We document a critical systems-level trade-off: explicitly bypassing the Quantized Johnson-Lindenstrauss (QJL) transform in the TurboQuant external vector database. While QJL mathematically guarantees unbiased inner products, we demonstrate that its added variance fatally harms nearest-neighbor retrieval precision at ultra-low bit-widths, making raw MSE quantization superior for retrieval stability. We also transparently profile the hardware tax of this pipeline, noting a 36.8% reduction in generation speed when decoding 110,000-token contexts due to polar-to-Cartesian un-rotation. 

**3. The "Anti-Memorization" NeedleChain Evaluation Protocol**
We move beyond the fundamentally flawed "Needle-In-A-Haystack" (NIAH) benchmark—which primarily rewards superficial positional encodings—by evaluating our architecture on the highly adversarial NeedleChain and LongBench v2 datasets. To rigorously prove that our system overcomes the "Lost in the Middle" (LIM) syndrome, we introduce the **Anti-Memorization Audit**: a logging protocol that tracks every single active retrieval trigger. Any correct answer outputted by the pipeline that bypassed a necessary retrieval step for buried evidence is explicitly flagged as parametric memorization and marked as a failure. This guarantees that our reported metrics reflect genuine, unbroken inferential logic over dense 50-100 paper corpora.

**4. Fully Localized Adaptation of Corrective RAG (CRAG)**
As a comparative baseline (Track A), we successfully adapt the Corrective Retrieval-Augmented Generation (CRAG) routing logic for strictly local, air-gapped deployments. We utilize a highly memory-efficient 0.77B T5-Large evaluator to score retrieved document relevance. We modify the standard CRAG architecture by removing its reliance on commercial web search APIs for "Ambiguous" (0.5 to 0.9 confidence) and "Incorrect" (<0.5 confidence) triggers. Instead, we successfully redirect these triggers to execute broader vector searches over the local 100-paper corpus and implement a "decompose-then-recompose" knowledge strip filter for "Correct" matches (>0.9 confidence), preventing API dependencies and securing the local-deployment constraint.
