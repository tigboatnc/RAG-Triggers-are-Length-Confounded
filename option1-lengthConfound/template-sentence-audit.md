## ⚠ NARRATIVE-DEVELOPMENT AUDIT (added pass)

> **Big-picture diagnosis.** The paper currently *announces* findings ("we present", "nine signals beat", "FCT exceeds the baseline") rather than *driving the reader toward* them. Each section starts with its discourse-marker topic sentence and lists evidence; the *cause-and-effect arcs* that would let a reviewer feel narrative momentum (e.g., "length fails on the held-out cells *and therefore* FCT shines there"; "lift_em isolates causality *and therefore* the published pool's `closed_em` results are doubly confounded") exist in the paper but are scattered across §3.1, §4.2, §4.3, §5.1, and §5.2 — the reader has to *assemble* the argument rather than *follow* it. Reference points scanned during this audit: Janiak's *Illusion of Progress* (EMNLP 2025), Vashurin *UNCERTAINTY-LINE* (arXiv 2505), Datta *Decide Early and Explain Later* (ICML 2026), Santilli *Revisiting UQ Evaluation* (ACL 2025), plus OpenReview review threads on `Verbosity≠Veracity` (ICLR 2025 submission, rejected), `CoCoA` (NeurIPS 2025 poster), and `FLARE` (EMNLP 2023). The dominant reviewer-attack pattern across these is **definition/operationalization mismatch** (the paper's defined construct ≠ the empirically computed quantity) followed by **missing-comparison baseline** (relevant prior work not contrasted). Our paper survives both attacks but the *defenses* are not where the *attacks* will land.

### Six narrative gaps identified (in priority order)

1. **`lift_em=+1` defense lives at §5.1 but the attack lands in §3.1.** A hostile reviewer reads §3.1 S5–S6, sees "this is a strictly different question" + the *tautological* "by construction a retrieval trigger," and concludes the paper's target choice is unjustified. The defense — `lift_em` cleanly **isolates the causal retrieval-helpfulness signal from the confounded length-correlated incorrectness signal** — exists in §5.1's causal chain framing but is not delivered until the reader has spent 3 pages mounting the attack. **Fix:** pull a half-sentence ("`lift_em` isolates the **causal** retrieval-helpfulness from the **confounded** length-correlated incorrectness target that the published trigger pool actually evaluates against") into §3.1 S5/S6 merged paragraph + Abstract S3 + §1 p3 S1. User concern #1, load-bearing.

2. **"First length-controlled audit" reads as overclaim against Vashurin.** Vashurin's UNCERTAINTY-LINE *is* the residualization protocol — they applied it to NMT/summarization/math UQ. We are the first to apply it to the dynamic-RAG trigger pool. A reviewer who has read Vashurin will see our "first length-controlled audit" as either un-crediting or overclaiming. **Fix:** localize the "first" claim to *the application* — "the first audit of the **dynamic-RAG trigger pool** under `\citet{vashurin_uncertainty-line_2025}`'s length-residualization protocol" — in Abstract S3, §1 p3 S1, §1 I1. User concern #2.

3. **FCT differentiation from Zhang-commit-2026 lives only in App. E Table 5.** A casual reader of §3.3 S4 will see FCT as "Datta's $t^\star$ with the argmin sign flipped." The operational differentiation along three load-bearing axes (sampling regime $K{=}5$ vs $K{=}1$, trace model class non-thinking vs thinking, application target retrieval-triggering vs early-stopping or pre-verbalization) is in the appendix. **Fix:** promote one row of App. E inline to §3.3 S4: *"FCT operates on $K{=}5$-majority answer extracts in non-thinking-mode chat models, where neither Datta's `</think>`-anchored forced completion nor Zhang's finite-set continuation projection applies directly"* — plus mirror the half-sentence in §1 p3 S3. User concern #3.

4. **K=5 recommendation is self-defeating without the capture-time defense.** §3.3 S5 and §4.5 S2 say "K=5 is null vs K=1; we recommend K=1 for deployment." A reviewer asks: "if K=1 is equivalent, why didn't you re-run the audit with K=1?" The honest answer (the K=5 capture was already on disk when the ablation came back null) is **not in the paper**. **Fix:** one sentence in §4.5 S2 — *"The headline audit reports $K{=}5$ because the capture protocol was already $K{=}5$ when the ablation returned null; the equivalent $K{=}1$ protocol would be cheaper to re-run from scratch but would not change the survivor matrix at our power."* User concern #4.

5. **§4 Results is "we just dump numbers."** Each subsection (4.1–4.6) starts with its finding stated flat, and the inter-subsection narrative arc (length-orthogonal survivors exist → length itself fails on the hardest cells → FCT shines exactly there → robustness to judge / K / dataset) is left for the reader to assemble. The reader follows the structure but never feels driven. **Fix:** one transition sentence per subsection (5 sentences total) that connects (i) the result of the previous subsection to (ii) what the current subsection adds. Highest *narrative* ROI in the paper.

6. **Abstract S5 ↔ S6 reorder.** The inversion finding (length anti-predictive on held-out 2Wiki) is the harder-to-explain-away result and does the most narrative work for a length-confound paper. Currently S5 leads with FCT (the named contribution); S6 lands the inversion. Swapping leads with the *finding* (inversion) and reveals the *named contribution* (FCT) second — Janiak's abstract-style framing. Lower-stakes than gaps 1–5; recommended.

# Title (`title`)

> **Narrative goal of the title.** The title is the paper's promissory note: a reader's first 0.5 s decision is "Do I open this PDF?" For a length-confound paper competing with Vashurin's (which explicitly names the protocol `UNCERTAINTY-LINE`) and Santilli's (which names the *finding* "Spurious Interactions with Response Length"), our title needs to do three things in one phrase: (i) name the protocol scope (length-controlled), (ii) name the object class (dynamic-RAG triggers), and (iii) signal that this is an *evaluation*, not a new method. The current title does all three; the question is whether "Length-Controlled" carries enough mechanical hint that residualization is involved (Vashurin's title does this with "Length-Invariant").

- [ ] **T1.** Length-Controlled Evaluation of Training-Free Dynamic-RAG Triggers
  - ✓ **Title vs. claims.** Title makes four implicit promises, each cashed elsewhere: (1) **length-controlled** = the residualization protocol of §3.2 (Eq. 2). # Abstract

> **Narrative goal of the abstract.** A short paper's abstract is one paragraph that has to do four jobs in roughly four sentences each: (1) **establish the gap** (length-confounding is documented in neighboring literatures but not yet measured in the dynamic-RAG pool), (2) **state what we did** (length-residualized audit on a paired-oracle target), (3) **lead with the strongest non-obvious finding** (FCT beats length by +0.190, the strongest length-orthogonal trigger; raw length is *anti*-predictive on the hardest cells), (4) **certify robustness** (judge-equivalence preserves the survivor set, FCT *strengthens* under the judge). The current abstract executes (1)–(4) but each sentence reads transactionally — "X, then Y, with number Z" — rather than building the reader toward a surprise. Reference points: Santilli's abstract drives toward "AUROC is systematically skewed under length confound" as the *finding*; Vashurin's drives toward "even normalized measures still leak length"; Janiak's drives toward "simple length heuristics rival complex detectors." All three lead with the *finding*, not the method. Our strongest finding is the **inversion on held-out cells** (Abstract S6) — that's the buried lede a reviewer will value because it's the empirical anchor for *why* this paper matters. Reorder S5 ↔ S6 so the inversion lands before FCT, and tighten S5's two-clause numerical dump.

- [ ] **S1.** Dynamic retrieval-augmented generation systems decide *when* to retrieve from a scalar trigger signal, scored by raw AUROC against binary correctness.
  - ✓ **Framing claim — uncited prose.** This is descriptive of standard practice; the supporting citations are deferred to `intro.p1 S5` ("All seven follow the same evaluation protocol... rank traces by the trigger score; report raw AUROC..."). No citation required at the abstract level; matches standard practice in DRAGIN, FLARE, SeaKR, INSIDE, ETC, RSUS papers.
  - **Narrative improvement.** The opening sentence frames the *protocol* but not the *stake*. A reader who isn't already in the length-confound community sees "decide when to retrieve" as a banal restatement of dynamic RAG. The stake is that the trigger decides whether the system pays the retrieval tax or not, on every query. Suggested punch-up: *"Dynamic retrieval-augmented generation systems pay the retrieval tax only on queries whose scalar trigger signal exceeds a threshold; the published triggers are scored by raw AUROC against binary correctness."* The "tax" framing makes the cost concrete and immediately signals why a confound matters — a length-tracking trigger overpays on long-trace queries and underpays on short-trace queries.
