\begin{abstract}
Dynamic retrieval-augmented generation systems decide \emph{when} to retrieve from a scalar trigger signal, scored by raw AUROC against binary correctness. A converging recent literature has shown this protocol is length-confounded on adjacent QA selective-prediction and UQ tasks, yet most dynamic-RAG trigger evaluations still use it. We present the first length-controlled audit of the published training-free dynamic-RAG trigger pool under length-residualized AUROC on a paired-oracle target ($\texttt{lift\_em}{=}+1$: retrieval rescue). On two open-weight chat models over two multi-hop QA datasets ($n{=}800$ paired traces), nine signals beat the length baseline at 95\% paired-bootstrap, and four survive Bonferroni at $k{=}15$ across four signal families. We propose \textbf{FCT} (first-commitment time), a forward-looking variant of the answer-trajectory primitive: high on traces where the model commits to its final answer only late in the decode. FCT exceeds the length baseline by $\Delta = +0.190$ [+0.093, +0.287] (Bonferroni lower bound $+0.047$), the strongest length-orthogonal signal. On the held-out 2Wiki cells, raw answer length is \emph{anti}-predictive of retrieval need (AUROC $\leq 0.52$), inverting the verbosity-compensation pattern observed on single-hop QA. The survivor set is \textbf{robust to a semantic-equivalence judge}: under a GPT-4o-Mini correctness function agreeing with EM at $\kappa = 0.707$, eight of nine survivors are preserved and FCT \emph{strengthens} to $\Delta = +0.222$. Length-residualized and raw AUROC disagree most on the cells where retrieval matters most, motivating both protocols in dynamic-RAG trigger comparisons on multi-hop QA.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Dynamic retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems retrieve external evidence during decoding to ground generation in source material \citep{lewis_retrieval-augmented_2021, trivedi_interleaving_2023, shao_enhancing_2023}. The control point of every such system is the \emph{retrieval trigger}: a scalar function of the partial generation that decides when to invoke retrieval. The published pool of training-free triggers spans five signal families that each read a different decode-time observable: attention-based DRAGIN \citep{su_dragin_2024} (token importance from attention dynamics), token-probability FLARE \citep{jiang_active_2023} (next-token entropy / max log-prob at the decode head), hidden-state SeaKR \citep{yao_seakr_nodate} (internal-state covariance across $K$ samples), multi-sample text-consistency SelfCheckGPT \citep{manakul_selfcheckgpt_2023} (cross-sample answer agreement on the generated text), and entropy-trend ETC \citep{li_modeling_2026} (second-difference of step entropy). All five follow the same evaluation protocol --- rank traces by the trigger score and report raw AUROC against a binary correctness label --- and all predate the length-confound literature. 

Recent work shows that length confounds the standard protocol. \citet{santilli_revisiting_2025} prove that shared length confounding systematically distorts AUROC, with Spearman $\rho = -0.7$ between length and Perplexity. \citet{janiak_illusion_nodate} report that switching ROUGE to LLM-as-Judge drops Perplexity AUROC by 45.9\% and EigenScore by 30.4\%; \citet{zhang_verbosity_2024} document the behavioral mechanism (verbose responses when uncertain) on the multi-hop QA family used here; and \citet{ielanskyi_addressing_2025} show that approximate correctness functions disagree on UQ rankings. \citet{vashurin_uncertainty-line_2025} propose \textsc{Uncertainty-Line}, a regression-residualization protocol applied to Neural Machine Translation (NMT), summarization, and mathematical reasoning; dynamic-RAG triggers remain an untested application domain.

We evaluate the published trigger pool under length-residualized AUROC against a paired-oracle target, $\texttt{lift\_em}{=}+1$, which labels each closed-book trace by whether re-decoding under gold context flips a wrong answer to a right one \citep{mallen_when_2023}. The paired design shares the same exact-match correctness function across both arms, which partially cancels the function-bias critique of \citet{ielanskyi_addressing_2025}. We introduce \textbf{FCT}, a forward-looking first-commitment-time signal in the recently-emerged answer-trajectory family \citep{datta_large_2026, wang_chain--probe_nodate, chen_i_2026}: FCT is the strongest length-robust trigger in the audited pool, exceeding the length baseline by $\Delta = +0.190$ AUROC (Bonferroni-significant at $k{=}15$). On the held-out 2Wiki cells, length is itself anti-predictive of retrieval need, inverting the verbosity-compensation pattern observed on single-hop QA.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
\item A length-controlled evaluation of the published training-free dynamic-RAG trigger pool (the first to our knowledge), identifying four length-robust signals from four distinct signal families not previously distinguished under the raw-AUROC protocol.
\item We introduce \textbf{FCT} (first-commitment time), a forward-looking signal in the answer-trajectory family, exceeding the length baseline by $\Delta = +0.190$ [+0.093, +0.287] AUROC (Bonferroni lower bound $+0.047$) and reaching raw AUROC \textbf{0.784} on the held-out Gemma$\times$2Wiki cell.
\item Cross-architecture and cross-domain validation on one dense (Qwen2.5-7B) and one MoE-hybrid (Gemma-4-26B-A4B-it) open-weight chat model over two multi-hop QA benchmarks ($n{=}800$ paired traces; base rate $13.5\%$): nine signals clear length at nominal 95\% paired-bootstrap and four survive Bonferroni at $k{=}15$.
\item A direct robustness check against the LLM-as-judge critique of \citet{janiak_illusion_nodate}: under a GPT-4o-Mini semantic-equivalence judge agreeing with EM at Cohen's $\kappa = 0.707$, eight of nine paired-significant survivors and all four Bonferroni-robust signals are preserved; FCT strengthens to $\Delta = +0.222$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Length confounds the standard AUROC protocol for LLM uncertainty quantification \citep{santilli_revisiting_2025, vashurin_uncertainty-line_2025, janiak_illusion_nodate, zhang_verbosity_2024, ielanskyi_addressing_2025}; \citet{mao_confidence_2026} note that joint generation probability suffers severe length bias. Step-level answer-trajectory triggers are an emerging length-orthogonal signal class \citep{datta_large_2026, wang_chain--probe_nodate, chen_i_2026, zhang_harnessing_2026, lu_streaming_2026}. The training-free trigger pool we audit spans attention-based DRAGIN \citep{su_dragin_2024} and DR-RAG \citep{hei_dr-rag_2024}, token-probability FLARE \citep{jiang_active_2023}, multi-sample text-consistency SelfCheckGPT \citep{manakul_selfcheckgpt_2023} and SAC$^3$ \citep{zhang_sac3_2023}, hidden-state INSIDE \citep{chen_inside_2024} and SeaKR \citep{yao_seakr_nodate}, and entropy-trend ETC \citep{li_modeling_2026}. Trained alternatives that require per-architecture supervised data \citep{asai_self-rag_2023, tang_mba-rag_2024, chen_think_2026, guo_dior_2025} fall outside our training-free scope. The conceptual parent of our paired-oracle target is \citet{mallen_when_2023}, who asks when retrieval helps a parametric model.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Paired-Oracle \texttt{lift\_em} Protocol}
\label{sec:methods-protocol}

For each question $q$ in the evaluation set, we run two greedy decodes on the same chat model: a closed-book decode that produces trace $\tau_c^{(q)}$ and answer $a_c^{(q)}$ with no retrieval context, and an oracle-retrieval decode conditioned on the gold supporting paragraphs that produces $a_o^{(q)}$. We extract all trigger signals from $\tau_c^{(q)}$; the oracle decode only labels the trace. The discrimination target is
\begin{equation}
\texttt{lift\_em}(q) = \mathbb{1}\!\left[\mathrm{EM}(a_o^{(q)}) > \mathrm{EM}(a_c^{(q)})\right],
\label{eq:lift-em}
\end{equation}
\emph{i.e.}, a trace receives label $+1$ exactly when the closed-book answer is wrong and the gold-context answer is right --- precisely the subpopulation where retrieval would convert a failure into a success. This is a strictly different question from the single-arm ``which traces are wrong?'' question that the published trigger pool actually evaluates against (and that length predicts moderately well on multi-hop QA; \S\ref{sec:discussion-causal}): we instead ask, of the queries that the model could not solve unaided, which ones would retrieval rescue? A signal that ranks $\texttt{lift\_em}{=}+1$ traces high is, by construction, a retrieval trigger --- thresholding it at a high percentile recovers exactly the retrieval-helpful subpopulation. The paired design shares the same exact-match correctness function across both arms, which partially insulates against the function-bias critique of \citet{ielanskyi_addressing_2025}. We evaluate on HotpotQA \citep{yang_hotpotqa_2018} and 2WikiMultiHopQA \citep{ho_constructing_2020} --- the latter held out for cross-dataset evaluation --- at $n{=}200$ paired traces per architecture-by-dataset cell. We select one dense (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) and one MoE-hybrid (Gemma-4-26B-A4B-it) open-weight chat model to test cross-architecture generality within a single 80\,GB GPU compute budget. The positive class is rare (108 of 800 paired traces, base rate $13.5\%$ across the headline pool).

\subsection{Length Residualization}
\label{sec:methods-residualization}

For each trigger signal $s$ on a cohort of paired traces, we compute the length-residualized score
\begin{equation}
s_{\text{resid}} = s - \hat{\alpha} - \hat{\beta}\,\log(\texttt{tokens} + 1),
\label{eq:residualize}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{\alpha}, \hat{\beta}$ are OLS coefficients fit on the cohort. $\mathrm{AUROC}(s_{\text{resid}})$ measures whether the signal carries information beyond length. We use $\log(\texttt{tokens}+1)$ rather than raw length $L$ because multi-hop reasoning traces have a long-tailed token distribution; a linear-in-$\log L$ regression is the natural homoscedastic counterpart of \citeauthor{vashurin_uncertainty-line_2025}'s linear-in-$L$ regression on their shorter NMT and summarization traces. Both parameterizations agree within $\varepsilon \leq 0.029$ AUROC against the public \textsc{Uncertainty-Line} reference implementation on both architectures (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-uline}); higher-order polynomial regressors give no further improvement, consistent with \citeauthor{vashurin_uncertainty-line_2025}'s App. D.1 finding that the linear fit is sufficient.

\subsection{FCT and the Answer-Trajectory Family}
\label{sec:methods-fct}

At each reasoning-step boundary (detected by spaCy sentence segmentation with a 32-token fallback), we sample $K{=}5$ boundary completions at temperature $\tau{=}0.6$, top-$p$~0.9, and extract per-step answers via a regex over the model's \texttt{Answer:} prefix. From the resulting trajectory $\mathcal{M} = (m_1, \ldots, m_n)$ of per-step $K{=}5$ majority answers, we define four trace-level aggregations:
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{flip\_rate}: fraction of consecutive-step majority disagreements;
\item \texttt{late\_flips}: same, restricted to the last half of the trace;
\item \texttt{min\_majority}: $1 - \min_t (\max_{\text{count}}/K)$, the worst within-step disagreement;
\item \textbf{FCT}, a forward-looking first-commitment time
\begin{equation}
\textsc{FCT} = \frac{\min \{ i : m_j = a_{\text{final}} \;\forall\, j \geq i \}}{\max(1, n - 1)} \in [0, 1].
\label{eq:fct}
\end{equation}
\end{itemize}
\noindent High FCT denotes extended deliberation: the trace has not converged on its final answer until late in the decode. These aggregations adapt the \texttt{AnswerSwitches} primitive of \citet{datta_large_2026} to non-thinking-mode chat models; Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-fct-tstar} tabulates the operational differences from Datta's $t^\star$. A $K{=}1$ vs.\ $K{=}5$ ablation of \texttt{flip\_rate} is null on both architectures (\S\ref{sec:results-ablation}), and we recommend $K{=}1$ for deployment efficiency.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Cross-Architecture Survivor Matrix}
\label{sec:results-matrix}

Nine signals clear length at nominal 95\% and four survive Bonferroni at $k{=}15$ across four distinct signal families (Figure~\ref{fig:survivor-forest}): attention-based DRAGIN-RIND ($\Delta = +0.167$), logit-family \texttt{ent.max} ($+0.137$), answer-trajectory \texttt{min\_majority} ($+0.149$), and the forward-looking commitment-time signal \textbf{FCT} ($+0.190$, the strongest length-robust trigger in the audited pool). Confidence intervals are paired-bootstrap at nominal 95\% and Bonferroni-corrected $\sim$99.67\% ($\alpha{=}0.0033$, $B{=}10{,}000$). The full table of all 17 signals is in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-hero}.

\subsection{Length Is Anti-Predictive on the Held-Out 2Wiki Cells}
\label{sec:results-inverted}

On the held-out 2Wiki cells (Qwen $\times$ 2Wiki and Gemma $\times$ 2Wiki), raw answer length is \emph{anti}-predictive of retrieval need: the \texttt{tokens\_generated} baseline reaches raw AUROC \textbf{0.457} and \textbf{0.518} on $\texttt{lift\_em}{=}+1$, both at or below chance. Longer traces on the held-out multi-hop cells are systematically \emph{less} retrieval-helpful --- a direct inversion of the verbosity-compensation pattern \citet{zhang_verbosity_2024} document on single-hop QA. The four Bonferroni-robust survivors clear the length baseline by a wider margin on these cells than on the HotpotQA cells, suggesting the raw-AUROC protocol miscredits the length signal on exactly the cells where retrieval matters most.

\subsection{FCT Discriminates Strongest on the Held-Out MoE Cell}
\label{sec:results-fct-percell}

On the same held-out cells where length fails (\S\ref{sec:results-inverted}), FCT discriminates most strongly --- reaching raw AUROC \textbf{0.784} on the Gemma~$\times$~2Wiki cell (Table~\ref{tab:fct-percell}). The architecture-by-dataset variance is consistent with cell-dependent retrieval-helpfulness structure (\S\ref{sec:discussion}): the cells where the length proxy collapses are precisely the cells where the answer-trajectory primitive carries the most independent information.

\subsection{Survivor Set Is Robust to a Semantic-Equivalence Judge}
\label{sec:results-llmjudge}

We addressed the correctness-function critique of \citet{janiak_illusion_nodate} and \citet{ielanskyi_addressing_2025} by relabelling both arms of every paired trace via a GPT-4o-Mini semantic-equivalence judge (Janiak's exact choice) and re-running the full survivor matrix. EM and the judge agreed at Cohen's $\kappa = 0.707$ (substantial agreement; \citealp{landis_measurement_1977}). Eight of the nine paired-significant survivors under EM remained significant under the judge, with one out-in swap (\texttt{etc} loses, \texttt{flare.max} gains) that does not affect the Bonferroni-robust four. FCT strengthened under the judge from $\Delta = +0.190$ [+0.093, +0.287] to $\Delta = +0.222$ [+0.132, +0.312] (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-llmjudge}).

\subsection{The $K{=}5$ Multi-Sample Variant Is Null}
\label{sec:results-ablation}

The $K{=}5$ boundary-completion variant carries no significant advantage over the single-sample $K{=}1$ proxy at our power: paired-bootstrap CIs straddle zero on both architectures (Table~\ref{tab:k-ablation}). We recommend $K{=}1$ for deployment at $5\times$ less compute per boundary, with equivalent triggering performance.

\subsection{Robustness Summary}
\label{sec:results-robustness}

The headline finding (four Bonferroni-robust signals with FCT strongest) is invariant under three independent design choices: (i) the choice of correctness function (EM vs.\ a GPT-4o-Mini semantic-equivalence judge agreeing with EM at $\kappa = 0.707$; \S\ref{sec:results-llmjudge}); (ii) the choice of sample size in the answer-trajectory family ($K{=}1$ vs.\ $K{=}5$ on \texttt{flip\_rate} is null on both architectures; \S\ref{sec:results-ablation}); and (iii) the choice of evaluation dataset (HotpotQA vs.\ held-out 2WikiMHQA; FCT discriminates strongest on the held-out Gemma~$\times$~2Wiki cell at raw AUROC \textbf{0.784}; \S\ref{sec:results-fct-percell}, \S\ref{sec:results-inverted}). The residualization protocol itself agrees with the \textsc{Uncertainty-Line} reference implementation of \citet{vashurin_uncertainty-line_2025} within $\varepsilon \leq 0.029$ AUROC on both architectures (App.~\ref{sec:appendix-uline}).


\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Why Length Predicts Retrieval-Helpfulness}
\label{sec:discussion-causal}

On the secondary $\texttt{closed\_em}{=}0$ target, \texttt{tokens\_generated} reached raw AUROC 0.539 on the headline pool and was the single strongest pre-residualization signal on several cells. This is consistent with the \emph{verbosity compensation} mechanism documented by \citet{zhang_verbosity_2024} across multi-hop QA, where verbose-when-uncertain is a real LLM behavior on the HotpotQA, MuSiQue, and 2WikiMHQA family. A plausible causal chain underlying this pattern is $\text{length} \rightarrow \text{verbosity compensation} \rightarrow \text{uncertainty} \rightarrow \text{incorrectness} \rightarrow \text{retrieval need}$: under this picture, length confounds the evaluation because it proxies the proximal cause, and the trigger pool's signals receive partial AUROC credit for tracking length because length correlates with the deeper uncertainty mechanism. Residualization (\S\ref{sec:methods-residualization}) removes the length pathway and isolates the residual signal.

\subsection{Why Answer-Trajectory Stability Predicts Retrieval Need}
\label{sec:discussion-fct}

The mechanism behind FCT reads naturally as \emph{premature commitment}, the interpretation \citet{datta_large_2026} give to their \texttt{AnswerSwitches} and $t^\star$ primitives: the model decides on an answer early and then continues to verbalize reasoning that supports the commitment, with most subsequent steps being post-decision tail. \citeauthor{datta_large_2026} apply this observation to early stopping (if the model has decided, no further compute is needed). Retrieval triggering reads the same primitive in the opposite causal direction: traces where the model has \emph{not} decided, where the answer trajectory continues to flip through the reasoning, are the traces where external evidence would resolve the uncertainty. The paired-oracle target $\texttt{lift\_em}{=}+1$ formalizes this question directly. The same primitive supports two complementary operations: detect stabilization to \emph{stop}; detect non-stabilization to \emph{retrieve}.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We evaluated the published training-free dynamic-RAG trigger pool under length-residualized AUROC on a paired-oracle target ($\texttt{lift\_em}{=}+1$). On two open-weight chat models over two multi-hop QA datasets, nine signals retained a length-orthogonal positive margin at nominal 95\% paired-bootstrap and four survived Bonferroni at $k{=}15$ across four distinct signal families. The strongest length-robust trigger is \textbf{FCT}, a forward-looking first-commitment-time signal in the answer-trajectory family. The survivor set is robust to a GPT-4o-Mini semantic-equivalence judge ($\kappa = 0.707$, eight of nine survivors preserved). We argue that length-controlled evaluation should be reported alongside raw AUROC in every dynamic-RAG trigger comparison.

\section*{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Signal-level audit, not end-to-end pipelines.} We audit trigger \emph{signals} as defined in their original papers, not full deployment pipelines (threshold tuning, retriever choice, prompt format). Our claims pertain to signal-level discriminative power on $\texttt{lift\_em}$.

\paragraph{Architecture and dataset coverage.} The headline pool covers two architectures (Qwen2.5-7B dense, Gemma-4-26B-A4B-it MoE-hybrid) and two multi-hop QA benchmarks. The cheapest extensions that would tighten the cross-architecture claim are a third open-weight chat model (e.g., Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) and a third multi-hop benchmark (MuSiQue, \citealp{trivedi_musique_2022}); we report the current Bonferroni lower bound on FCT ($+0.047$, App.~\ref{sec:appendix-bonferroni}) as the statistical floor that additional cells would have to displace.

\paragraph{Dataset framing: held-out, not OOD.} We refer to 2WikiMultiHopQA as a \emph{held-out} cell relative to HotpotQA, not an out-of-distribution benchmark in the strict sense: both are multi-hop QA over Wikipedia with comparable question complexity, and the cross-dataset gap is closer to a transfer-evaluation gap than the kinds of distribution shift studied in the formal OOD-detection literature \citep{lu_streaming_2026, ielanskyi_addressing_2025}. The cell-dependent length-behavior we report (\S\ref{sec:results-inverted}) is a finding about dataset-specific length-confound structure on multi-hop QA, not a claim about OOD generalization; a stricter cross-distribution evaluation (different task family, different domain, or a synthetic perturbation set) is future work.

\paragraph{Correctness function.} We use exact-match equality on both arms of the paired-oracle target; the paired design shares the same EM function across arms (\S\ref{sec:methods-protocol}), and Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-llmjudge} reports an LLM-as-Judge robustness check ($\kappa = 0.707$, eight of nine survivors preserved). Containment-match and $F_1$ variants remain future work.

\paragraph{Non-thinking-mode chat models.} The audited models are non-thinking-mode chat models. \citet{datta_large_2026} developed their original primitive on thinking-mode reasoning models with explicit \texttt{</think>} tokens; our $K{=}5$ boundary-completion adaptation is not byte-faithful to that protocol.

\paragraph{Integration as contribution.} Our contribution is the integration of length-residualized AUROC, the paired-oracle $\texttt{lift\_em}$ target, and the answer-trajectory family including FCT. Each piece individually is incremental over its parent; the integration into a single length-controlled audit, and the empirical identification of length-robust survivors, are the load-bearing claims.

\paragraph{Risks.} Our work concerns evaluation methodology for retrieval triggering, on open-weight chat models and public multi-hop QA benchmarks; we release no new datasets or trained models, and direct risks are limited. One methodological risk is that the length-residualized protocol could be cited in isolation to defend deployed dynamic-RAG systems whose triggers track length more strongly than residualization alone would reveal. We therefore recommend reporting raw, length-residualized, and LLM-judge-validated AUROC together (\S\ref{sec:results-llmjudge}, \S\ref{sec:results-robustness}) rather than substituting one for another.

\section{Correctness-Function Robustness: LLM-as-Judge}
\label{sec:appendix-llmjudge}

\paragraph{Protocol.} For each of the $n{=}800$ paired traces in the headline pool, we re-labelled both arms via an OpenAI \texttt{gpt-4o-mini} semantic-equivalence judge under a strict prompt (``You are a strict semantic-equivalence judge for multi-hop QA. Reply with exactly one word: YES or NO. No explanation.''). The judge target is
\begin{equation}
\texttt{lift\_em}_{\textsc{judge}}(q) = \mathbb{1}\!\bigl[\mathrm{judge}(a_o^{(q)}){=}\textsc{YES} \;\wedge\; \mathrm{judge}(a_c^{(q)}){=}\textsc{NO}\bigr].
\label{eq:lift-em-judge}
\end{equation}
We re-ran the full headline survivor matrix with paired bootstrap $B{=}10{,}000$.

\paragraph{Inter-label agreement.} EM identifies 108 helped traces and the judge identifies 113; the intersection is 87. The two labels jointly produce Cohen's $\kappa = 0.707$ over the 3-class $\{+1, 0, -1\}$ label set, in the substantial-agreement band of \citet{landis_measurement_1977}.

\paragraph{Survivor matrix transfer.} Table~\ref{tab:llmjudge} reports the headline survivor matrix under both correctness functions side-by-side. Eight of the nine paired-significant survivors under EM remained significant under the judge; \texttt{etc} loses significance and \texttt{flare.max} gains it. FCT strengthened under the judge from $\Delta = +0.190$ [+0.093, +0.287] to $\Delta = +0.222$ [+0.132, +0.312] ($+17\%$ in point estimate, $+42\%$ in 95\% CI lower-bound margin). All four Bonferroni-robust survivors retain nominal-95\% significance under the judge.
