\begin{abstract}
Dynamic retrieval-augmented generation systems decide \emph{when} to retrieve from a scalar trigger signal, scored by raw AUROC against binary correctness. A converging recent literature has shown this protocol is length-confounded on adjacent QA selective-prediction and uncertainty-quantification (UQ) tasks, where length itself rivals internal-signal triggers. We present the first length-residualized audit of the training-free dynamic-RAG trigger pool on a paired-oracle target ($\texttt{lift\_em}{=}+1$) that asks whether retrieval flips a wrong answer to a right one, isolating the causal retrieval-helpfulness signal from length-correlated incorrectness. Across two open-weight chat models on HotpotQA and 2WikiMultiHopQA ($n{=}800$ paired traces), nine signals beat the length baseline at 95\% paired-bootstrap and only four survive Bonferroni at $k{=}15$ across three families. On the held-out 2Wiki cells, raw length itself collapses to at-or-below chance (AUROC $\leq 0.52$), and on those same cells our \textbf{FCT} (first-commitment time, a forward-looking answer-trajectory signal) reaches raw AUROC $\textbf{0.784}$ on Gemma~$\times$~2Wiki, the strongest length-orthogonal trigger in the audited pool. The survivor set is robust to a GPT-4o-Mini semantic-equivalence judge ($\kappa = 0.707$ with EM), and FCT itself \emph{strengthens} from $\Delta = +0.190$ to $\Delta = +0.222$ under the judge, the opposite of what a string-level EM artifact would predict.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Dynamic retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems retrieve external evidence during decoding to ground generation in source material \citep{lewis_retrieval-augmented_2021, trivedi_interleaving_2023, shao_enhancing_2023}. At the core of every such system sits a \emph{retrieval trigger}: a scalar function of the partial generation that decides when to fire retrieval. We restrict our audit to \emph{training-free} triggers (those that decode without per-architecture supervised data, setting aside trained adapters such as Self-RAG, \citealp{asai_self-rag_2023}). Prior work spans six signal families: attention-based DRAGIN \citep{su_dragin_2024}, token-probability FLARE \citep{jiang_active_2023}, entropy-trend ETC \citep{li_modeling_2026}, hidden-state SeaKR \citep{yao_seakr_nodate} and INSIDE \citep{chen_inside_2024}, multi-sample text-consistency RSUS \citep{guo_realm_2026}, and hedging-marker SELFDOUBT-T1 \citep{pandey_selfdoubt_2026}. A contemporaneous answer-trajectory family tracks step-level dynamics of the model's own predicted answer \citep{datta_large_2026, wang_chain--probe_nodate, chen_i_2026}. All seven families share an evaluation convention inherited from the earliest peers (FLARE, DRAGIN): rank traces by the trigger score and report raw AUROC against a binary correctness label.

Recent work has shown this evaluation convention is length-confounded on adjacent QA selective-prediction and uncertainty-quantification (UQ) tasks. If the confound transfers to the dynamic-RAG pool, the published triggers' reported AUROC gains may partly reflect length tracking rather than genuine signal. \citet{santilli_revisiting_2025} prove formally that mutual length-bias on UQ scores and correctness functions systematically distorts AUROC, with Spearman $\rho = -0.7$ between token length and Perplexity. \citet{janiak_illusion_nodate} show the empirical consequence: detection-method AUROC drops by up to 45.9\% when switching from ROUGE to LLM-as-Judge labels on Mistral. \citet{zhang_verbosity_2024} attribute the underlying behavior to verbosity compensation on the LongBench multi-hop bundle (HotpotQA, MuSiQue, 2WikiMultiHopQA), and \citet{ielanskyi_addressing_2025} document a related correctness-function failure across approximate metrics. The methodological response is \citet{vashurin_uncertainty-line_2025}'s \textsc{Uncertainty-Line}, which regresses each UQ score on output length and uses the residual as a length-invariant estimate, validated on NMT, summarization, and mathematical reasoning. The dynamic-RAG trigger pool is the missing application.

We extend the length-residualization protocol of \citet{vashurin_uncertainty-line_2025} to the training-free dynamic-RAG trigger pool. Rather than score triggers against single-arm correctness, we re-decode each closed-book trace under gold context and ask whether retrieval flips a wrong answer to a right one, defining a paired-oracle target $\texttt{lift\_em}{=}+1$ conceptually motivated by \citet{mallen_when_2023}. This isolates the \emph{causal} retrieval-helpfulness signal from the length-correlated incorrectness signal that the published pool actually measures (\S\ref{sec:discussion-causal} develops the chain), and the shared exact-match function across both arms partially answers the function-bias critique of \citet{ielanskyi_addressing_2025}. We also propose \textbf{FCT} (first-commitment time), a forward-looking variant of the answer-trajectory primitive \citep{datta_large_2026, wang_chain--probe_nodate, chen_i_2026, zhang_commit_2026}. FCT reads $K{=}5$ majority answers from non-thinking-mode chat models, where neither Datta's \texttt{</think>}-anchored forced completion nor \citeauthor{zhang_commit_2026}'s finite-set continuation projection applies directly (App.~\ref{sec:appendix-fct-tstar}). FCT is the strongest length-orthogonal trigger in the audited pool ($\Delta = +0.190$ AUROC, Bonferroni-significant at $k{=}15$). On the held-out 2Wiki cells, length itself becomes anti-predictive of retrieval need, inverting the verbosity-compensation pattern \citet{zhang_verbosity_2024} document on adjacent QA tasks.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
\item The first length-residualized audit of the dynamic-RAG trigger pool, extending the protocol of \citet{vashurin_uncertainty-line_2025}, identifying four Bonferroni-robust signals from three families (attention, token-probability, answer-trajectory) not previously contrasted under this protocol.
\item \textbf{FCT} (first-commitment time), our forward-looking variant of the answer-trajectory primitive, exceeding the length baseline by $\Delta = +0.190$ AUROC (Bonferroni lower bound $+0.047$) and reaching raw AUROC \textbf{0.784} on the held-out Gemma$\times$2Wiki cell.
\item Cross-architecture validation on one dense (Qwen2.5-7B) and one MoE-hybrid (Gemma-4-26B-A4B-it) open-weight chat model over HotpotQA and 2WikiMultiHopQA, with nine signals clearing length at 95\% and four surviving Bonferroni at $k{=}15$.
\item Robustness against the correctness-function critique of \citet{janiak_illusion_nodate} and \citet{ielanskyi_addressing_2025}: under a GPT-4o-Mini judge agreeing with EM at Cohen's $\kappa = 0.707$, all four Bonferroni-robust signals are preserved and FCT strengthens to $\Delta = +0.222$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

The length-confound critique of standard UQ AUROC evaluation comes from QA UQ and hallucination detection \citep{santilli_revisiting_2025, janiak_illusion_nodate, zhang_verbosity_2024, ielanskyi_addressing_2025, vashurin_uncertainty-line_2025}, with \citet{mao_confidence_2026} extending the same observation to stepwise confidence signals. The answer-trajectory family \citep{datta_large_2026, wang_chain--probe_nodate, chen_i_2026} provides the parent primitives for FCT. Two related supervised methods sit outside our training-free scope: \citet{lu_streaming_2026} train hidden-state probes for streaming hallucination detection, and \citet{zhang_harnessing_2026} contrastively shape answer embeddings under latent perturbation at the trace boundary. We use ``answer-trajectory'' for step-to-step disagreement under $K{=}5$ boundary-completion sampling, distinct from Zhang's latent-perturbation ``answer agreement.'' Trained adaptive-retrieval policies \citep{asai_self-rag_2023, tang_mba-rag_2024, chen_think_2026, guo_dior_2025} likewise fall outside our audit. Our paired-oracle target operationalizes the question of when retrieval helps a parametric model \citep{mallen_when_2023}, replacing static entity-popularity proxies with per-trace closed-vs-oracle re-decode.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Paired-Oracle \texttt{lift\_em} Protocol}
\label{sec:methods-protocol}

For each question $q$, we run two greedy decodes on the same chat model: a closed-book pass that produces trace $\tau_c^{(q)}$ and answer $a_c^{(q)}$, and an oracle pass conditioned on the gold supporting paragraphs that produces $a_o^{(q)}$. We read all trigger signals from $\tau_c^{(q)}$. The oracle decode only supplies the label. The discrimination target is
\begin{equation}
\texttt{lift\_em}(q) = \mathbb{1}\!\left[\mathrm{EM}(a_o^{(q)}) > \mathrm{EM}(a_c^{(q)})\right],
\label{eq:lift-em}
\end{equation}
which flags traces where retrieval would convert a wrong answer to right. The published trigger pool instead targets single-arm correctness, which length predicts moderately well on multi-hop QA (\S\ref{sec:discussion-causal}). Our target asks whether retrieval would have rescued the trace, isolating the causal retrieval-helpfulness signal from the length-correlated incorrectness pathway. Sharing the same exact-match function across both arms additionally blunts the correctness-function critique of \citet{ielanskyi_addressing_2025}.

We evaluate on HotpotQA \citep{yang_hotpotqa_2018} and 2WikiMultiHopQA \citep{ho_constructing_2020}, holding the latter out as a cross-dataset cell, at $n{=}200$ paired traces per architecture-by-dataset cell. The two models are one dense (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) and one MoE-hybrid (Gemma-4-26B-A4B-it), chosen for cross-architecture coverage within a single 80\,GB GPU budget. The positive class is rare: 108 of 800 paired traces, base rate $13.5\%$.

\subsection{Length Residualization}
\label{sec:methods-residualization}

We adopt the residualization protocol of \citet{vashurin_uncertainty-line_2025}. For each trigger signal $s$ on a cohort of paired traces, we fit OLS of $s$ on $\log(\texttt{tokens}+1)$ and evaluate AUROC on the residual:
\begin{equation}
s_{\text{resid}} = s - \hat{\alpha} - \hat{\beta}\,\log(\texttt{tokens} + 1).
\label{eq:residualize}
\end{equation}
$\mathrm{AUROC}(s_{\text{resid}})$ then measures whether the signal carries information beyond length. We use $\log(\texttt{tokens}+1)$ rather than the raw $L$ of their shorter NMT and summarization traces because multi-hop reasoning traces are long-tailed, and a linear-in-$\log L$ fit matches that geometry. On both architectures the two parameterizations agree within $\varepsilon \leq 0.029$ AUROC against the public \textsc{Uncertainty-Line} reference implementation (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-uline}). We adopt the linear fit, consistent with their App.~D.1 polynomial sweep finding the linear case sufficient.

In their originating papers, ETC, FLARE, the SeaKR primitive, and SELFDOUBT-T1 are online or per-token signals. We evaluate each as a trace-level summary statistic, with granularity adapters detailed in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-adapters}.

\subsection{FCT and the Answer-Trajectory Family}
\label{sec:methods-fct}

At each reasoning-step boundary (detected by spaCy sentence segmentation with a 32-token fallback), we sample $K{=}5$ boundary completions at temperature $\tau{=}0.6$, top-$p$~0.9, and extract a per-step answer from each via a regex over the model's \texttt{Answer:} prefix. From the resulting per-step $K{=}5$ majority sequence $\mathcal{M} = (m_1, \ldots, m_n)$ we compute four trace-level aggregations:
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{flip\_rate}: fraction of consecutive-step majority disagreements;
\item \texttt{late\_flips}: count of consecutive-step disagreements between the per-step $K{=}5$ answer \emph{sets}, restricted to the last half of the trace (a raw-incidence counterpart to the fraction-based \texttt{flip\_rate});
\item \texttt{min\_majority}: $1 - \min_t (\max_{\text{count}}/K)$, the worst within-step disagreement;
\item \textbf{FCT}, a forward-looking first-commitment time
\begin{equation}
\textsc{FCT} = \frac{\min \{ i : m_j = a_{\text{final}} \;\forall\, j \geq i \}}{\max(1, n - 1)} \in [0, 1].
\label{eq:fct}
\end{equation}
\end{itemize}
\noindent High FCT denotes late convergence: the trace has not settled on its final answer until close to the end of the decode. All four aggregations adapt the \texttt{AnswerSwitches} primitive of \citet{datta_large_2026} to non-thinking-mode chat models with $K{=}5$ majority-answer extracts, a regime in which neither Datta's \texttt{</think>}-anchored forced completion nor \citeauthor{zhang_commit_2026}'s finite-set continuation projection applies directly (App.~\ref{sec:appendix-fct-tstar} tabulates the operational differences along five axes). The audit reports $K{=}5$ because that was the capture protocol when the $K{=}1$ vs.\ $K{=}5$ ablation came back null (\S\ref{sec:results-ablation}). Deployment can use $K{=}1$ for $5\times$ less compute per boundary without changing the survivor matrix.

\paragraph{CABV (continuous answer-belief velocity).} CABV is a continuous between-step generalization of \texttt{flip\_rate}. At each populated step $t$, we build the Laplace-smoothed empirical distribution $p_t$ over the union of $K{=}5$ answers ever observed in the trace, and average the Jensen--Shannon divergence between consecutive populated steps,
\begin{equation}
\textsc{CABV} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}|-1}\sum_{t \in \mathcal{P}} \mathrm{JSD}(p_t, p_{t+1}),
\label{eq:cabv}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{P}$ is the set of populated step indices. CABV reduces to \texttt{flip\_rate} when each step concentrates on a single answer, and is closest in spirit to the inference-time instability signal of \citet{chen_i_2026} (defined on JSD between token distributions rather than $K{=}5$ answer distributions).

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Cross-Architecture Survivor Matrix}
\label{sec:results-matrix}

Of the 16 audited signals, nine clear the length baseline at nominal 95\% paired-bootstrap, and only four survive Bonferroni correction at $k{=}15$ (Figure~\ref{fig:survivor-forest}): attention-based DRAGIN-RIND ($\Delta = +0.167$), token-probability \texttt{ent.max} ($+0.137$), answer-trajectory \texttt{min\_majority} ($+0.149$), and \textbf{FCT} ($+0.190$, the strongest length-orthogonal trigger in the pool). The four survivors span three of the seven declared families, which suggests that the length-orthogonal signal is not a quirk of any one mechanism. Two families have no nominal-95\% survivor: hidden-state (SeaKR, INSIDE, the CRV variants) and hedging-marker (SELFDOUBT-T1) are entirely absorbed by length under our protocol, in line with their high length-correlation per cell (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-spearman}). The full table is in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-hero}.

\subsection{Length Is Anti-Predictive on the Held-Out 2Wiki Cells}
\label{sec:results-inverted}

The Bonferroni-robust four answer one question: which signals beat length? A sharper question is when length itself stops being informative. On the two held-out 2Wiki cells, raw answer length is at or below chance for predicting retrieval need: \texttt{tokens\_generated} reaches AUROC \textbf{0.457} on Qwen~$\times$~2Wiki and \textbf{0.518} on Gemma~$\times$~2Wiki. The verbosity-compensation pattern \citet{zhang_verbosity_2024} document on the same LongBench bundle does not transfer to \texttt{lift\_em}: longer multi-hop traces are no longer reliably retrieval-helpful, consistent with our framing in \S\ref{sec:methods-protocol} that \texttt{lift\_em} measures retrieval-rescue rather than length-correlated incorrectness. The four Bonferroni-robust survivors clear the length baseline by a wider margin on these cells than on HotpotQA, meaning the raw-AUROC protocol miscounts the length signal on exactly the cells where retrieval matters most.

\subsection{FCT Discriminates Strongest on the Held-Out MoE Cell}
\label{sec:results-fct-percell}

The cells where length fails are the cells where FCT succeeds. On Gemma~$\times$~2Wiki, the cell with the worst length proxy (\texttt{tokens\_generated} 0.518), FCT reaches raw AUROC \textbf{0.784}, a $+0.266$ absolute gap over length (Table~\ref{tab:fct-percell}). The same pattern repeats on Qwen~$\times$~2Wiki: where the length proxy is below chance (0.457), FCT's margin ($+0.147$) is wider than on either HotpotQA cell. We attribute this architecture-by-dataset variance to cell-dependent retrieval-helpfulness structure (\S\ref{sec:discussion}): the answer-trajectory primitive carries the most independent information where the length proxy collapses.

\subsection{Survivor Set Is Robust to a Semantic-Equivalence Judge}
\label{sec:results-llmjudge}

The natural alternative explanation for the survivor matrix is that it reflects EM's string-matching artifact rather than a length-orthogonal signal. To rule this out we relabelled both arms of every paired trace via a GPT-4o-Mini judge (\citeauthor{janiak_illusion_nodate}'s exact choice) and re-ran the audit, addressing the correctness-function critique of \citet{janiak_illusion_nodate} and \citet{ielanskyi_addressing_2025}. EM and the judge agree at Cohen's $\kappa = 0.707$ (substantial agreement; \citealp{landis_measurement_1977}). Eight of the nine paired-significant survivors under EM remain significant under the judge, with a single swap (\texttt{etc} loses, \texttt{flare.max} gains) that leaves the Bonferroni-robust four intact. FCT itself \emph{strengthens} under the judge from $\Delta = +0.190$ to $\Delta = +0.222$, the opposite of what a string-level EM artifact would predict under semantic relabeling (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-llmjudge}).

\subsection{The $K{=}5$ Multi-Sample Variant Is Null}
\label{sec:results-ablation}

A second design choice worth stress-testing is the $K{=}5$ sampling regime in the answer-trajectory family. On both architectures, the paired-bootstrap CI on $\Delta_{K{=}5 - K{=}1}$ for \texttt{flip\_rate} straddles zero (Table~\ref{tab:k-ablation}). The audit reports $K{=}5$ throughout because that was the capture protocol when the ablation came back null. Re-running at $K{=}1$ would not change the survivor matrix at our power, and for deployment $K{=}1$ delivers the same triggering performance at $5\times$ less compute per boundary.

\subsection{Robustness Summary}
\label{sec:results-robustness}

The headline finding survives three modal critiques a length-confound reviewer would raise. On the correctness-function critique of \citet{janiak_illusion_nodate} and \citet{ielanskyi_addressing_2025}, EM and the judge agree at $\kappa = 0.707$ and FCT strengthens under the judge (\S\ref{sec:results-llmjudge}). On the sampling-regime ambiguity in the answer-trajectory family \citep{datta_large_2026, wang_chain--probe_nodate}, the $K{=}1$ vs.\ $K{=}5$ comparison is null on both architectures (\S\ref{sec:results-ablation}). On the dataset-cherry-picking critique, FCT discriminates strongest on the held-out Gemma~$\times$~2Wiki cell at raw AUROC \textbf{0.784} (\S\ref{sec:results-fct-percell}, \S\ref{sec:results-inverted}). The residualization protocol itself matches the public \textsc{Uncertainty-Line} reference implementation within $\varepsilon \leq 0.029$ AUROC on both architectures (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-uline}).


\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Why Length Predicts Retrieval-Helpfulness}
\label{sec:discussion-causal}

On the secondary $\texttt{closed\_em}{=}0$ target, \texttt{tokens\_generated} reaches raw AUROC $0.539$ on the headline pool, competitive with several internal signals. This pattern matches \citet{zhang_verbosity_2024}'s verbosity-compensation mechanism: longer responses when uncertain are a real LLM behavior on multi-hop QA. A plausible causal chain is $\text{length} \rightarrow \text{verbosity compensation} \rightarrow \text{uncertainty} \rightarrow \text{incorrectness} \rightarrow \text{retrieval need}$. Length predicts $\texttt{closed\_em}{=}0$ moderately well because it proxies the upstream incorrectness step. The paired-oracle target $\texttt{lift\_em}{=}+1$ strips that step by conditioning on closed-book wrongness, which is why length collapses on the held-out 2Wiki cells (\S\ref{sec:results-inverted}). Residualization (\S\ref{sec:methods-residualization}) provides the complementary control on any candidate trigger.

\subsection{Why Answer-Trajectory Stability Predicts Retrieval Need}
\label{sec:discussion-fct}

The mechanism behind FCT reads naturally as \emph{premature commitment}, the interpretation \citet{datta_large_2026} give to their \texttt{AnswerSwitches} and $t^\star$ primitives. The model decides on an answer early and continues to verbalize reasoning that supports the commitment, with most subsequent steps as post-decision tail. \citeauthor{datta_large_2026} apply this observation to early stopping: if the model has decided, no further compute is needed. Retrieval triggering reads the same primitive in the opposite causal direction. The traces where the model has not decided, where the answer trajectory continues to flip through the reasoning, are exactly the traces where external evidence would resolve the uncertainty. The paired-oracle target $\texttt{lift\_em}{=}+1$ formalizes that question directly. The same primitive supports two complementary operations: detect stabilization to \emph{stop}, detect non-stabilization to \emph{retrieve}.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented the first length-residualized audit of the dynamic-RAG trigger pool, extending the protocol of \citet{vashurin_uncertainty-line_2025} to a paired-oracle target ($\texttt{lift\_em}{=}+1$). On two open-weight chat models over two multi-hop QA datasets, nine signals beat the length baseline at 95\% paired-bootstrap and four survived Bonferroni at $k{=}15$ across three families. The strongest length-orthogonal trigger is \textbf{FCT}, our forward-looking variant of the answer-trajectory primitive, exceeding length by $\Delta = +0.190$ AUROC and reaching raw $0.784$ on the held-out Gemma~$\times$~2Wiki cell. The survivor set is robust to a GPT-4o-Mini semantic-equivalence judge ($\kappa = 0.707$), with FCT itself strengthening under the judge. The residualization plus paired-oracle target combination is post-hoc and pool-agnostic, and we expect it to apply to other UQ-trigger evaluations beyond multi-hop QA.

\section*{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Signal-level audit, not end-to-end pipelines.} We audit trigger \emph{signals} as defined in their original papers, not full deployment pipelines (threshold tuning, retriever choice, prompt format). Our claims pertain to signal-level discriminative power on $\texttt{lift\_em}$.

\paragraph{Bonferroni knife-edge.} Two of the four Bonferroni-robust survivors carry $\sim$99.67\% lower bounds within $5{\times}10^{-3}$ of zero (\texttt{ent.max} LB $= +0.001$; \texttt{min\_majority} LB $= +0.004$, App.~\ref{sec:appendix-bonferroni}). The FCT and DRAGIN-RIND lower bounds are robust at $+0.047$ and $+0.039$. The four-survivor count is therefore fragile to small perturbations: dropping \texttt{ent.max} leaves three signals across two families, dropping \texttt{min\_majority} leaves three across three. We report this transparently rather than tightening $B$ to widen the margin.

\paragraph{Architecture and dataset coverage.} The headline pool covers two architectures (Qwen2.5-7B dense, Gemma-4-26B-A4B-it MoE-hybrid) and two multi-hop QA benchmarks. The cheapest extensions that would tighten the cross-architecture claim are a third open-weight chat model (e.g., Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) and a third multi-hop benchmark (MuSiQue, \citealp{trivedi_musique_2022}). We report the current Bonferroni lower bound on FCT ($+0.047$, App.~\ref{sec:appendix-bonferroni}) as the statistical floor that additional cells would have to displace.

\paragraph{Dataset framing: held-out, not OOD.} We treat 2WikiMultiHopQA as a \emph{held-out} cell relative to HotpotQA, not as out-of-distribution in the strict sense: both are multi-hop QA over Wikipedia with comparable question complexity. The cell-dependent length behavior (\S\ref{sec:results-inverted}) is a dataset-specific finding on multi-hop QA rather than a claim about OOD generalization, and a stricter cross-distribution evaluation is future work.

\paragraph{Correctness function.} We use exact-match equality on both arms of the paired-oracle target. The shared EM function across arms (\S\ref{sec:methods-protocol}) and the LLM-as-Judge robustness check (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-llmjudge}; $\kappa = 0.707$, eight of nine survivors preserved) partially insulate the headline against this choice. Containment-match and $F_1$ variants remain future work.

\paragraph{Non-thinking-mode chat models.} The audited models are non-thinking-mode chat variants. \citet{datta_large_2026} developed their answer-trajectory primitive on thinking-mode reasoning models with explicit \texttt{</think>} tokens. Our $K{=}5$ boundary-completion adaptation is not byte-faithful to that protocol.

\paragraph{Integration as contribution.} Our contribution is the integration of length-residualized AUROC, the paired-oracle $\texttt{lift\_em}$ target, and the answer-trajectory family including FCT. Each piece individually is incremental over its parent. The integration into a single length-residualized audit, and the empirical identification of Bonferroni-robust survivors, are the load-bearing claims.

\paragraph{Risks.} Our work concerns evaluation methodology for retrieval triggering on open-weight chat models and public multi-hop QA benchmarks. We release no new datasets or trained models, and direct risks are limited. One methodological risk is that the length-residualized protocol could be cited in isolation to defend deployed dynamic-RAG systems whose triggers track length more strongly than residualization alone would reveal. We therefore recommend reporting raw, length-residualized, and LLM-judge-validated AUROC together (\S\ref{sec:results-llmjudge}, \S\ref{sec:results-robustness}) rather than substituting one for another.

\section{Correctness-Function Robustness: LLM-as-Judge}
\label{sec:appendix-llmjudge}

\paragraph{Protocol.} For each of the $n{=}800$ paired traces in the headline pool, we re-labelled both arms via an OpenAI \texttt{gpt-4o-mini} semantic-equivalence judge under a strict three-sentence system prompt: ``You are a strict semantic-equivalence judge for multi-hop QA. Your task is to decide whether a model-predicted answer is semantically equivalent to a gold answer. Reply with exactly one word: YES or NO. No explanation.'' The judge target is
\begin{equation}
\texttt{lift\_em}_{\textsc{judge}}(q) = \mathbb{1}\!\bigl[\mathrm{judge}(a_o^{(q)}){=}\textsc{YES} \;\wedge\; \mathrm{judge}(a_c^{(q)}){=}\textsc{NO}\bigr].
\label{eq:lift-em-judge}
\end{equation}
We re-ran the full headline survivor matrix with paired bootstrap $B{=}10{,}000$.

\paragraph{Inter-label agreement.} EM identifies 108 helped traces and the judge identifies 113; the intersection is 87. The two labels jointly produce a multi-class Cohen's $\kappa = 0.707$ on the 3-class $\texttt{lift\_em} \in \{+1, 0, -1\}$ label set, in the substantial-agreement band of \citet{landis_measurement_1977}.

\paragraph{Survivor matrix transfer.} Table~\ref{tab:llmjudge} reports the headline survivor matrix under both correctness functions side-by-side. Eight of the nine paired-significant survivors under EM remain significant under the judge, with a single swap: \texttt{etc} loses significance and \texttt{flare.max} gains it. FCT strengthens under the judge from $\Delta = +0.190$ [+0.093, +0.287] to $\Delta = +0.222$ [+0.132, +0.312] ($+17\%$ in point estimate, $+42\%$ in 95\% CI lower-bound margin), the opposite of what a string-level EM artifact would predict. All four Bonferroni-robust survivors retain nominal-95\% significance under the judge.

\section{Adapter Notes}
\label{sec:appendix-adapters}

We audit signal-level discriminative power on \texttt{lift\_em} (\S\ref{sec:limitations}). Several primitives in the published trigger pool are originally framed as online per-token triggers or per-trace scores, and we evaluate their underlying statistic at the trace level. The per-signal notes below state the original primitive, our trace-level adaptation, and any sign or granularity drift.

\paragraph{ETC \citep{li_modeling_2026}.} The original ETC operates on a token-level entropy stream; we evaluate $\Delta^2 H$ on the per-step mean entropy series captured at boundary-detection events (\S\ref{sec:methods-fct}). We score $\max |\Delta^2 H|$ rather than the signed second-difference, so the adapter is magnitude-faithful but not sign-faithful.

\paragraph{FLARE \citep{jiang_active_2023}.} The original FLARE is an online per-token retrieval trigger ($\max_t p_t < \tau$); we audit the underlying primitive (low-confidence detection) as a trace-level summary $\max$ over steps of $1 - \min p_t$. The trigger \emph{policy} is not audited; the trigger \emph{statistic} is.

\paragraph{SeaKR \citep{yao_seakr_nodate}.} We use $K{=}5$ EOS hidden states for compute efficiency, smaller than the paper's reported best-$k$ interval $K \in [10, 25]$. The regularized Gram determinant we compute is the same primitive as the paper's self-aware uncertainty score. INSIDE \citep{chen_inside_2024} is the closest peer, using the EigenScore on the centered sample covariance; the $K$ nonzero eigenvalues of our uncentered Gram and INSIDE's centered covariance are rank-equivalent up to centering.

\paragraph{SELFDOUBT-T1 \citep{pandey_selfdoubt_2026}.} HVR $= h/(v+1)$ is computed per step and aggregated as $\max$ over steps; the original paper computes a single trace-level HVR with a Tier-1 ``HVR=0'' high-precision correctness gate. The per-step adapter is recoverable as the paper's gate by setting our threshold to $0$.

\paragraph{Concurrent commitment-time primitives.} Operational differences between \citet{datta_large_2026}'s $t^\star$, \citet{zhang_commit_2026}'s $\tau_{\text{commit}}$, and our FCT are tabulated in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-fct-tstar}.
